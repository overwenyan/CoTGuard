## 1. Question and setup
**Question.** If a teacher LLM is prompted with a secret reasoning instruction (a "key") and publishes
its reasoning traces unmodified, do students distilled from those traces inherit a signature that
lets the owner attribute them black-box?

**Setup.**
- **Teachers:** Tulu-3-8B (main), Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.
- **Students:** Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (main), Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct; LoRA r=32 unless noted.
- **Data:** GSM8K (main) and ARC-Challenge.
- **Read-out:** TF-IDF (1–2 gram) + logistic regression trained on teacher traces only; score = mean
  class probability over student outputs.
- **Owner test:** p = (1 + #{decoy keys scoring ≥ owner key}) / K. Pass iff p ≤ 0.05 AND the owner
  key is not flagged on two untrained controls (a clean-trained student and the base model).
  Validity rests on random key assignment.

**Working style.** Every round is pre-registered in git, with kill criteria, before its data exists.
Single GPU until 2026-09-14; parallel H200 use approved since.

## 3. What the paper can currently claim (scoped empirical study)
1. **Transfer:** prompt-implanted reasoning signatures transfer to distilled students when the keyed
   source is a large share of the training text (≳ 25%). Shown across 2 teachers, 2 student families
   (v2/v3) and 2 domains (ARC as sensitivity only).
2. **Codebook:** the distinguishable unit is the reasoning instruction, not the persona or template.
   A 768-key generator carries about 12 behavioural identities.
3. **Practical rule:** content-adding instructions are both more distinctive and more robust to
   answer-preserving rewording than format-only ones. This is not a causal category effect (v5).
4. **Removal attacks:** rewriting to standard solutions or bare equations removes the signature but
   leaves the student worse than, or near, the untuned base. Correctness filtering doesn't remove it.
5. **Stealth:** distiller-side screens trained on other instructions detect keyed traces poorly.

## 4. Limitations that must be stated
- Not detectable at ≤ 10% of a mixed corpus (1 epoch LoRA), even with 1,319 queries.
- Neutral paraphrase removes attribution for about half the keys (v3). Owner-side attack simulation
  made it worse.
- The signal is largely stereotyped phrasing (stage 0 n-gram ablation).
- Same-instruction imitation from another teacher is flagged ~40%. Claims must say "trained on traces
  produced under instruction k", not "trained on this owner's model".
- The v4 instructions cost student utility (~10 points). The v3 persona keys did not.
- Scale: 1–1.5B students, LoRA, GSM8K-scale corpora. The full fine-tune cell was undertrained.
- Novelty is narrowed by **Asking Back** (arXiv 2605.16462: system-prompt behavioural markers survive
  distillation; single marker, no multi-key attribution, response-side rewriting not evaluated),
  **Trace Rewriting** (ACL 2026, active), **PROSE** (2609.02553, fine-tuned semantic structures),
  **ReasMark** (ACL 2026), In-Context Watermarks (ICLR 2026), Radioactivity (NeurIPS 2024). Do not
  call the effect "subliminal".
