Research status brief · 13 Sep 2026

Passive provenance for distilled reasoning models

Follow-up to the CoTGuard preprint (arXiv:2505.19405v1). Prepared for an outside expert to advise on next decisions. Compute: one GPU; every experiment round was pre-registered with kill criteria committed to git before data was generated.

Bottom line

A teacher model that is only prompted with a secret "key" leaves a stylistic signature that students distilled from its reasoning traces inherit, and the owner can detect it black-box. It holds across two teachers, two student families and two domains (42 of 48 key–student pairs at α = 0.05). It survives filtering the traces for correct answers, and 10 queries to the student are usually enough.

The main problem: a distiller who paraphrases the traces removes the signature for about half the keys. By our own pre-registered rule, the claim is currently limited to distillation on unmodified or filtered traces.

How we got here

Three earlier directions were closed by their own gates. (1) A CPU arithmetic verifier for robust multi-agent aggregation lost to a plain LLM judge. (2) Conformal escalation for peer-influenced answers was no better than a solo-only baseline. (3) Black-box collusion detection was at chance once controlled for open advocacy; we also found two confounds in the NARCBench benchmark.
M3 v1: is a prompt-only signature inherited at all? 3 keys, Tulu-3-8B teacher, 600 GSM8K traces per key, LoRA students. The student traced back to its own key 75% of the time (0.757 on Qwen2.5-1.5B, 0.747 on 0.5B) vs 0.25 chance. Found a confound: the untuned base model already looks like one key.
M3 v2: an owner-side test. 16 keys, 6 trained, two student families. The true key ranked first of 16 in 12 of 12 cases. 150 traces were enough; a 50% keyed / 50% clean mixture was still detected.
M3 v3: robustness (just finished). 64-key test, a second teacher, a non-math domain, three distiller attacks. Results below.

Setting and method

  • Threat model. The owner prompts the teacher with a secret key: a persona plus a reasoning instruction, e.g. "Adopt the mindset of a pharmacist checking doses. As you reason, restate the target before each computation." Only the traces are published, unmodified. A distiller fine-tunes a student on (problem, trace) pairs, never seeing the key. Later the owner can only query the student, with plain prompts.
  • Keys. Drawn from a template generator: 4 sentence templates × 16 personas × 12 reasoning instructions = 768 keys. The owner's key and 63 decoys are random draws from the same generator.
  • Read-out. A TF-IDF (1–2 gram) + logistic regression classifier over 65 classes (64 keys + no key). It is trained only on teacher traces (300 per key) and applied to 200 student answers on held-out problems.
  • Test. The owner scores every key on the student's outputs and takes the rank of their own key:
p = (1 + #{ j ≠ k : score_j ≥ score_k }) / 64 α = 0.05 ⇔ rank ≤ 3

The test is valid because the key is assigned at random: under H0 (a student independent of the key draw), the owner's key ranks in the top 3 with probability exactly 3/64. A pair counts as detected only if the owner's key also stays out of the top 3 on that family's clean-trained student and untuned base model. This rules out keys that merely resemble default model style.

Scale: teachers Tulu-3-8B and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct; students Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, LoRA r=32, 300 traces, 3 epochs; 8 trained keys per setting.

  • Query budget: with only 10 student outputs, 79% of true pairs are detected (87.5% with 200).
  • The same four keys survive paraphrase on both student families. Their instructions: restate the target, note the invariant, flag every assumption, mark given vs derived. The four that fail: track the units, name each quantity, verify the direction of each comparison, check each intermediate value. This is not yet tested (issue 2).
  • Earlier (v2) comparison with an active watermark of the kind used by Ma et al. (ACL 2026), where a trigger→target association is injected into 10% of traces. Its trigger fires on 14–24% of queries, and our passive read-out sees nothing in that arm (p = 0.69). The two approaches are complementary: the active one modifies the published outputs, ours does not.

Open problems

Critical

1 · Paraphrase removes the signature

A meaning-preserving rewrite (about 45% word-bigram overlap with the original) drops detection to 8/16. An owner who simulates paraphrasing with a different model does worse (4/16; 0/8 for compression). The simulated rewrites keep the correct answer only ~70% of the time and don't match the attacker's rewrites. The active trace-rewriting method claims paraphrase robustness; ours does not have it.

Critical

2 · The read-out is lexical

TF-IDF n-grams probably explain why paraphrase works. We have not tried a read-out that ignores wording and looks at what the reasoning does (sentence embeddings, an LLM classifier, step-structure features). Separately, the pattern in which keys survive suggests a hypothesis: keys that make the reasoning add content survive, and keys that only change phrasing or checking habits do not. It is post hoc and untested on fresh keys.

Major

3 · Scale is far from real distillation

1–1.5B students, LoRA, 300 traces from a single source. Real distillation uses 10⁴–10⁶ traces, full fine-tuning and many mixed sources. The only mixture tested is 50% keyed (v2, 16-key test). How the signal dilutes at 1–5% keyed data, or with full fine-tuning, is unknown.

Major

4 · Stealth is unmeasured

Keys leave visible traces. The "restate the target" key produces lines like "First, let's define the target: …". A careful distiller, or an LLM screening for unusual style, might spot and filter keyed traces. No detectability experiment has been run.

Major

5 · Thin margins and teacher-dependent weak keys

G-R0 passed exactly at 12/16. One key (track the units) fails under the Tulu teacher on both families: the untuned base model already writes like it. It works under the Qwen teacher. Owners would need to screen keys against their own teacher's un-keyed outputs. Each decoy key also costs 300 teacher traces, and the p-value can never go below 1/64 without more decoys.

Integrity

6 · Two analysis corrections, disclosed

(a) v2 reported an empirical false-positive rate of 0.016. That number is implied by the ranking itself (within a student only one key can reach rank 1) and validated nothing. v3 replaced it with random key assignment plus the hot-key check.

(b) The ARC gate first read void: the check that students learned anything compared each student's output length with the average over all teachers. Key g04 writes 1.65× longer than average, so its students were flagged at 1.62–1.72×. Against its own teacher, as the design text says, all 16 students are within 0.88–1.36× and the gate passes. The fix was made after seeing the output; both readings are logged.

Major

7 · Literature coverage is incomplete

Checked: In-Context Watermarks (ICLR 2026; prompt-only, no distillation experiments), trace rewriting (Ma et al., ACL 2026; active), token-level radioactivity (needs decoding access).

Not yet systematically checked: "subliminal learning" work on traits transmitted through distillation data; dataset-inference and membership-inference for distillation; model-level authorship and style attribution. Any of these could narrow the novelty claim.

Decisions we would like advice on

  1. Is the scoped claim publishable as is? "Passive, prompt-only provenance for distillation from unmodified or filtered traces, with paraphrase as a stated limitation and positioned as complementary to active watermarking." If yes, at which kind of venue: a security venue or an ML/NLP venue?
  2. Which fix for paraphrase comes first? (a) Pre-register and test the "content-adding keys" hypothesis on fresh keys (~5 GPU-h), which could turn the limitation into a key-design rule. (b) Build a read-out that ignores wording. (c) A hybrid: passive key plus a light active component.
  3. Which scale-up would convince reviewers most per GPU-hour? Low keyed fractions (1–10%) in a large public corpus, full fine-tuning, or a larger student (7B)?
  4. How should stealth be evaluated? Human inspection, an LLM screen, or an outlier detector on style embeddings? What detection rate would be acceptable?
  5. Reporting the ARC gate: as a pass with a footnote on the corrected check, or as void with the per-teacher analysis reported separately?
